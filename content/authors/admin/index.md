<p>I study how language reflects changing understandings of socially important concepts, groups, and public issues. My work brings together <strong>natural language processing, computational social science, and social psychology</strong> to investigate changes in meaning, framing, and social evaluation.</p>

<p>I am a postdoctoral researcher in <a href="https://www.frermann.de/">Prof. Lea Frermann's</a> Cognition and Communication Lab at the University of Tübingen, where I am developing research on how frames and social representations change over time — and what such shifts reveal about wider cultural and psychological change. I am also a co-investigator on <a href="https://mindshiftresearch.github.io/"><strong>MindShift</strong></a>, a project funded by Riksbankens Jubileumsfond (2027–2029) investigating how psychiatric terms are used and interpreted across clinical and everyday contexts, and whether language models can recover their intended meanings and emotional significance.</p>

<p>I received my PhD from the University of Melbourne in 2026, under the supervision of <a href="https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/profile/6837-nick-haslam">Prof. Nick Haslam</a> and <a href="https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/profile/738094-ekaterina-vylomova">A/Prof. Ekaterina Vylomova</a>, where I led the development of <a href="https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.76/"><strong>SIBling</strong></a> and <a href="https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.570/"><strong>LSC-Eval</strong></a>, computational frameworks for measuring and evaluating lexical semantic change, with social science applications. I also co-developed <a href="https://www.changeiskey.org/publication/2026-acl/2026.acl.pdf"><strong>SenseRel</strong></a>, a benchmark for relations between word senses, and have conducted research on mental health language, stigma, identity, and dehumanization.</p>
