<p>I study how language reflects and shapes the ways people understand social groups and issues over time. My research brings together <strong>natural language processing, computational social science, and social psychology</strong> to investigate changes in meaning, framing, and social evaluation.</p>

<p>I am a postdoctoral researcher in <a href="https://www.frermann.de/">Prof. Lea Frermann's</a> Cognition and Communication Lab at the University of Tübingen. My current research examines how social groups and public issues are framed across time, and what such changes can—and cannot—tell us about broader cultural change.</p>

<p>I received my PhD from the University of Melbourne in September 2026. In my doctoral research, I co-developed <strong>SIBling</strong> and <strong>LSC-Eval</strong>, frameworks for measuring and evaluating lexical semantic change, and co-developed <strong>SenseRel</strong>, a benchmark of semantic relations. I also study mental health language, stigma, identity, and dehumanization in public discourse.</p>
